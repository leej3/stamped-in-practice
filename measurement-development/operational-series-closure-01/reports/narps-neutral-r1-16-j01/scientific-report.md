## Scientific assessment

**Scientific adequacy: supported.** The report supports its bounded conclusion that these supplied group maps did not provide whole-brain-corrected evidence for a positive vmPFC gain effect. It correctly keeps that outcome distinct from evidence that the true response is zero. The analysis uses 54 equal-indifference participants, unthresholded participant COPEs, a positive one-sample voxelwise t test (df=53), and Bonferroni family-wise correction across 210,968 voxels. Nine positive voxels survived, all reported outside the vmPFC proxy; the proxy average was near zero with a 95% interval spanning zero. These methods address the specified group-stage hypothesis and the original whole-brain correction requirement. The reported outcome is **not supported** (no corrected vmPFC positive finding), which is not the same as scientific inadequacy.

Evidence: `content/workspace/report.md` lines 9–13 and 21–23; `content/workspace/analysis/group_analysis.py` (model, mask, correction, ROI and QC calculations); `content/workspace/results/summary.json`; and `content/workspace/results/fwe_positive_voxels.csv` (all nine corrected coordinates, each marked outside the proxy). The methods appropriately treat COPEs as parameter estimates, not monetary-unit slopes; note the separate gain/loss upstream model and missing first-level variance maps; and limit inference to the supplied upstream model. The atlas-derived vmPFC definition is defensible and described as a proxy. Bonferroni is conservative but valid for whole-brain family-wise control.

Limitations qualify the scope, not the reported no-support conclusion: the mask includes voxels nonzero in at least 90% of maps, so zero values in the remaining maps enter as observations rather than being modeled as missing; the report does not separately characterize that small amount of coverage variation. The Harvard–Oxford atlas is fetched at runtime rather than retained and pinned, which limits exact reproduction of the ROI boundary. No first-level variance maps are available. These limits argue against interpreting the result as proof of no vmPFC response or a response-per-currency-unit effect. No replay was performed, so the retained outputs were assessed as supplied results, not as replay evidence.
