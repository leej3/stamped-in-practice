# Round one: useful scientific work, with a confounded workflow comparison

All twelve workers completed the same NARPS group-analysis task, and all sixteen assigned independent assessments completed.
One additional repair assessment remains supplementary.
The explicit STAMPED workers received stronger primary workflow ratings and all three received supported scientific-adequacy judgments, but they also had a longer median session.
This small development round does not establish that the skills caused better science: common instructions already required much of the target workflow, assessment infrastructure changed during the round, and independent judges disagreed on several valid items.

The useful result is a set of complete research objects, a bounded scientific finding, and concrete changes to the next comparison.
Preserve this entire round as development evidence.
Do not pool it with a subsequent tool-neutral setup as a clean skill progression.

## What was compared

The four assistance strategies each had three independent workers: baseline, general skill discovery, scientific/reproducibility skill discovery, and explicit pinned STAMPED guidance. All used the same scientific inputs, model/runtime settings and original launch source.
Skills were fixed at `c90252a7`; the allocation and duplicate selection preceded launches.
The 54 supplied maps represent participants in the NARPS equal-indifference group, with preprocessing and individual modeling fixed upstream.
The question was whether potential gain has a positive group-level response in vmPFC under whole-brain-corrected inference.

This baseline was constrained.
Every worker was instructed to use Pixi, preserve a lock on a separate branch, write into a prescribed results location, use Duct and publish annex content.
Those requirements made workflow practices common inputs rather than effects attributable to assigned skills.
Coordinator retrieval, retention, checkpoint publication and later packaging are separate actions and earn no worker credit.

The first primary assessments for workers 07 and 08 used the original assessor.
The other ten primaries and four preselected duplicates used the declared transport/comparator amendment.
Worker 07's repair attempt did not replace its original primary or its allocated duplicate.
Exact slots, source versions, output hashes and evidence-specific validity overlays are in the disposition and extraction tables.
Neither favorable outcomes nor successful replay determined inclusion.

## Scientific result and its limits

Every worker reported no whole-brain-corrected positive vmPFC finding in the supplied gain-effect maps.
This is an absence of support under the chosen group models and anatomical definitions, not evidence that the biological effect is exactly zero or a replication of every upstream NARPS analysis.

The workers used different anatomical definitions, including atlas regions, spheres and coordinate restrictions.
Their reported regional means ranged from approximately −0.011 to +0.012 upstream model units, and all twelve descriptive 95% intervals included zero.
These regional intervals are not the required whole-brain-corrected tests.
The rows analyze the same participants and different regions; averaging them or treating them as independent study replications would be misleading.

![Reported regional estimates](../synthesis/figures/reported-regional-estimates.png)

Primary scientific judgments were six qualified, five supported and one not supported.
The three baseline analyses were qualified; general discovery had one supported and two qualified; science discovery had one of each; all three explicit STAMPED analyses were supported.
A supported judgment concerns adequacy of the analysis and its limited conclusion, not support for the positive hypothesis.

The reservations matter.
For example, the first worker's custom sign-flip implementation held a variance term fixed, while its conservative parametric primary analysis supplied separate evidence.
The adverse judgment for worker 08 raised image-coverage and zero-filling concerns.
Anatomical choices, missing-data treatment, upstream scaling and the scope of the reported inference remained scientific questions even when execution succeeded.
These are judge-reported findings, retained with their cited evidence rather than independently regraded here.

## Workflow evidence, replay and judgment variability

The item-level view shows more complete recorded workflow evidence for explicit STAMPED, especially workers 11 and 12.
It also shows residual environment and recurring-validation gaps.
There is no summed quality score: the checklist's MUST, SHOULD and MAY categories have different purposes, and tool-specific probes and evidence availability limit interpretation.

![Checklist evidence](../synthesis/figures/checklist-evidence.png)

Ten of the 372 primary item ratings are unavailable in the comparable tables because their cited measurement depended on failed assessor infrastructure.
The raw ratings remain present.
Eight of twelve primary replay measures are unavailable: two lost the authorized annex route; six selected required participant metadata for deletion as if it were a generated output.
In two affected attempts, a Duct log-prefix collision occurred first.
That observed worker limitation remains documented, while the malformed test cannot establish complete replay success or failure.
Documentary checklist ratings independent of the failed attempt remain usable.

The four interpretable primary replays reproduced scientific artifacts on the same host but received partial overall helper results.
Differences included run timing, generated prose or missing nonnumerical artifacts; these are not interchangeable with changed scientific estimates.
The successful selected-output comparisons establish their documented scope, not complete workflow cleanliness or portability to another machine.
Independent coordinator recipients retrieved every original stopped object; that preservation does not retroactively make a worker's publication complete.
Missing worker payloads for 06, 08 and 10 were preserved and uploaded separately by the coordinator.

The same-version duplicate assessments disagreed on 6 of 29 mutually valid checklist items for worker 03, 6 of 30 for worker 04, and 9 of 30 for worker 11.
Worker 07's pair crosses assessment versions and is not an agreement estimate.
Raw scientific labels were qualified/supported for 03 and supported/supported for 04 and 11, but each pair had uncertain dependence on unavailable replay evidence in one assessment; do not turn these into a calibrated scientific agreement rate.
The item differences justify keeping judgments and evidence visible rather than treating one aggregate grade as truth.

Total native worker-session time was 224.01 minutes, primary assessment time 110.06 minutes, duplicate time 33.53 minutes and supplementary repair time 6.75 minutes.
These are sums of elapsed session times, not elapsed project duration, CPU time or provider billing.
Parallel sessions overlap.
Nested review is already inside worker wall time; its token usage is retained separately and is not assumed included in the root usage counters.
Coordinator preparation, debugging, preservation and publication are substantial additional effort, not a measured zero.
Replay durations sit inside assessment sessions and must not be added again.

Installed skills are not evidence of application.
Baseline records show no observed skill selection or use.
General-discovery workers installed and read PDF guidance, but failed downloads or lack of subsequent application limited what the records establish.
Science-discovery worker 07 loaded notebook, validation and visualization guidance and made observed validation/figure changes; 08's PDF workflow failed at retrieval, and 09's loaded notebook/PDF guidance did not establish application.
Explicit workers loaded the pinned research guidance; 11 and 12 also retained independent review children and self-assessment artifacts.
These are observed actions, not proof that any single instruction caused an outcome.

Context isolation was incomplete.
Assignment metadata was visible in the assessed object even though judge prompts omitted treatment labels.
Some worker process listings exposed other run identifiers and, for baseline workers 01/02, condition arguments.
Worker 11's primary judge also made an initial wrong-default clone before selecting the intended object; the retained exposure record distinguishes fetched state from observed reads.
These limitations remain visible without inventing a behavioral effect or changing assigned conditions.

## Reflection and the next test

The central methodological lesson is to separate coordinator preservation from worker instructions.
The next setup should provide equivalent scientific resources, preserve every original delivery centrally, and assess equivalent scientific evidence across tools.
A baseline should not be told to adopt the very practices whose added value is being tested.
Assessment changes and common-instruction changes define a new setup, not a skill-only improvement.

The research guidance needs narrower decision rules.
Check custom inferential code against an independent formula or trusted implementation; distinguish missing coverage from genuine zero values; compare scientific quantities separately from timestamps and presentation; and select fresh review or replay in proportion to the unresolved scientific risk.
A documentary correction should not automatically trigger another analysis, review and assessment cycle.
Preserve exact inputs and environments through the chosen workflow, while accepting scripts, notebooks and other usable delivery forms.

These changes will be tested prospectively on new workers, after the assessor and common task/resources are stable.
Preserve unfavorable, null, interrupted and unavailable outcomes.
Predeclare the new replication and stopping plan; do not extend or select trials to obtain favorable scores.
Transfer beyond this one study remains untested.
