## Scientific assessment

The worker reports 54/54 participants retained and 198,029 voxels in the intersection mask. The one-sided positive one-sample t analysis yields 11 voxels significant after Bonferroni correction, but none in the operational vmPFC sphere centered at MNI (0, 40, -10). The sphere mean is −0.00552 cope units (95% CI −0.03094 to 0.01990; *t*(53)=−0.435; one-sided *p*=.667). The strongest corrected voxel is posterior at MNI (−26, −102, −3.6). Those results do not support the positive vmPFC hypothesis; the confidence interval includes small effects in both directions.

The method is defensible for the supplied group-stage inputs: participant maps are independent estimates, one-sample t tests are suitable for an unweighted group summary in the absence of participant variance maps, and Bonferroni controls whole-brain familywise error under arbitrary voxel dependence. The sphere is a transparent, plausible anatomical summary; it is not presented as an atlas-defined boundary. The task's normalized gain coefficient is correctly not interpreted per currency unit, and upstream processing is treated as fixed.

Qualification is needed because support is defined by nonzero map values rather than independent coverage masks, so an exact zero estimate cannot be distinguished from an out-of-coverage zero. The report does not provide a per-participant ROI coverage audit. Inference is therefore bounded to the common nonzero support used by the analysis. The group t model also relies on independent participants and an adequately behaved sampling distribution; it cannot model first-level precision without the absent variance maps. These limitations do not reverse the reported corrected result, but narrow what its absence means.
