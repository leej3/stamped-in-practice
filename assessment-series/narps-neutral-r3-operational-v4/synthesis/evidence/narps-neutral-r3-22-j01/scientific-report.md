## Scientific adequacy: **supported**

The reported conclusion is that the data do not support a positive gain-related vmPFC response under the required whole-brain family-wise error criterion. I judge that conclusion **supported** by the reported analysis and saved outputs, not as proof of a zero effect. The report describes all 54 equal-indifference participants, an unweighted one-sample random-effects t test of participant COPE estimates, a one-sided 4,999-draw participant sign-flip maximum-t correction over 198,029 common-support voxels, and an anatomically defined 10 mm sphere centered at MNI [0, 40, -12]. The saved summary gives a vmPFC sphere mean of -0.00544 COPE units (95% t interval [-0.03461, 0.02372]), with ROI peak t=1.70 and max-t FWE p=1.00. There are 24 FWE-significant voxels in three small posterior/inferior cerebellar components, not in the vmPFC sphere. The participant scatter and corrected map are consistent with the numerical report. These observations are supported by `content/workspace/report.md`, `content/workspace/analysis/analyze.py`, `content/workspace/results/summary.tsv`, `content/workspace/results/fwe_clusters.tsv`, `content/workspace/results/input_qc.tsv`, and `content/workspace/figures/vmPFC_effects.png` plus `group_t_map_fwe.png` in the original capture.

The group model is appropriate for independent participant-level effect estimates when participant variance maps are unavailable. The sign-flip maximum statistic addresses the directional hypothesis while controlling whole-brain FWE, as required; this approach presumes independent participants and a symmetric null distribution of effects, which the report states. The sphere is defined independently of the observed effects, and its ROI summary is described as descriptive rather than replacing corrected whole-brain inference. The report also correctly distinguishes the first-level gain COPE from a response per dollar: the upstream gain regressor was mean-centered and variance-normalized, and the separate gain/loss models are fixed inputs. It limits inference to the supplied AO86 pipeline and notes that missing first-level variance maps prevent carrying individual-model uncertainty into group analysis.

One material assumption is that exact zeros encode out-of-coverage voxels. `analysis/analyze.py` sets common support as `np.all(x3 != 0, axis=0)`; no separate coverage mask is supplied. This produces a constant-n intersection and is defensible for zero-filled backgrounds, as the report states, but the delivery does not independently verify that every zero means missing coverage rather than a genuine zero estimate. I therefore read the whole-brain mask and ROI numbers conditional on that convention. This limitation does not overturn the reported near-zero ROI estimate or its lack of corrected support. The report's statement that no corrected vmPFC support does not establish an exactly zero true effect is appropriately cautious.

**Scientific hypothesis conclusion (verbatim from the captured report):** “do **not** support a positive gain-related response in vmPFC under the required whole-brain family-wise error (FWE) criterion.” This is the report's outcome statement; it is separate from the adequacy category above.
