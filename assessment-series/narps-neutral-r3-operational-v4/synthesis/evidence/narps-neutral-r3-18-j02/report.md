## Scientific assessment

**Scientific adequacy: qualified.** The reported outcome is: “This group analysis does not support a positive gain effect in vmPFC.” This is a suitably cautious conclusion about the observed analysis, rather than a claim of zero effect. The report recognizes that input maps are participant contrast estimates rather than BOLD change per dollar, all 54 participants are retained, geometry and checksums are checked, and the vmPFC mask is specified independently of the tested maps. It reports a participant-level ROI mean of −0.003, 95% t interval [−0.028, 0.022], and one-sided p=.595, and explains why this is not evidence of equivalence. The design, interpretation, and caveats are documented in `content/workspace/report.md` and `content/workspace/analysis/protocol.md`.

The main whole-brain result needs qualification. In `content/workspace/analysis/run_analysis.py`, sign-flipped participant maps are divided by the standard error estimated from the original signs and held fixed for all flips. A conventional studentized sign-flip max-T test recomputes the standard error after each flip; the fixed denominator is not invariant to sign changes because the sample variance depends on the signed sample mean. Thus the reported max-T family-wise p-values and the count/localization of corrected voxels are not established by the stated permutation calibration. This weakens the primary whole-brain evidence required by the task. Separately, the 90%-coverage mask includes 12,939 voxels with only 49–53 nonzero maps; those missing map locations enter the group array as zeros. Within the 3,384 supported vmPFC voxels, 350 have fewer than 54 nonzero maps. The report notes the coverage threshold but does not model these locations as missing, so the ROI and voxelwise estimates may be affected. These issues make the conclusion conditional and limit its whole-brain support; the near-zero ROI summary and cautious “does not support” statement remain directionally compatible with no demonstrated positive effect. No alternative analysis was substituted.

Other strengths and limits: the independent Harvard–Oxford label combination and coordinate restriction are stated, but atlas-to-map localization crosses MNI template variants and is approximate as the report acknowledges. Equal weighting is reasonable with independent participant maps and no variance maps, but offers no precision weighting. Images and participant-level ROI estimates are inspectable. `results/validation.json` checks arithmetic and storage tolerance, not the validity of the permutation design. The supplied upstream model is fixed and not revalidated. A lack of support here does not establish equivalence, a zero effect, or absence in individuals.

The reported hypothesis outcome and scientific adequacy are separate: the worker reports no support for a positive vmPFC effect; the assessment of that reasoning is **qualified** because the primary correction and partial-coverage handling leave material uncertainty.
