## Scientific assessment

**Scientific adequacy: supported for the reported, bounded conclusion.** The report's conclusion is that the supplied maps do not provide whole-brain-corrected support for a positive vmPFC gain effect; it explicitly does not claim that the underlying effect is exactly zero. The group analysis includes all 54 equal-indifference participants and tests their participant-level gain COPEs with a positive-direction one-sample t statistic. Participant-wise sign flips and the maximum t statistic across the common nonzero mask provide whole-brain family-wise error control at .05 (`analysis/analyze.py`, `results/summary.json`). The method and its symmetric-null assumption are stated (`report.md`).

**Reported hypothesis outcome:** no corrected support for the positive vmPFC hypothesis. There were no positive FWE-significant voxels in the independently specified 10 mm sphere at MNI (0, 40, -10). The descriptive ROI mean was -0.0061 COPE units, with a 95% t interval [-0.0342, 0.0221] and one-sided uncorrected p=.666. The 24 positive FWE-significant voxels were posterior (strongest peak t(53)=6.62, empirical pFWE=.001), outside the vmPFC region (`results/summary.json`). The report and figures distinguish this posterior result from the vmPFC question (`report.md`, `figures/group_gain_vmPFC.png`, `figures/voxel_t_distribution.png`).

The inference is conditional on the fixed AO86 participant models. The upstream gain modulator was centered and variance-normalized, so these COPEs are not effects per currency unit; gain and loss were modeled separately, and variance maps were unavailable (`input/upstream-methods.md`). The worker identifies the common nonzero support mask as a way to avoid treating missing coverage as measured zero, and reports matching image shape/affine, finite values, no exclusions, and ROI outlier checks (`analysis/analyze.py`, `results/participant_qc.csv`). The complete-case spatial mask may reduce coverage and coverage was not separately visualized per participant. Sign-flipping relies on independent participants and a sufficiently symmetric null; the worker states this. These limits weaken interpretation beyond “no corrected evidence here,” but do not undermine that reported conclusion. The ROI interval is not an equivalence test.

The primary figure is readable and shows the mean effect, corrected positive map, and participant ROI values. Its brain panels are a single axial slice, so localization relies on the accompanying coordinates and the broader report; it is not a whole-brain anatomical rendering.
