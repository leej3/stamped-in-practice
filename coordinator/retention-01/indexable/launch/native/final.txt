## Result

The analysis **does not support a positive gain effect in vmPFC after whole-brain correction**. In the 54-participant equal-indifference group, no voxel in the anatomically defined vmPFC ROI survived max-t family-wise error correction. The ROI mean was 0.012 cope units (95% CI −0.008 to 0.033; descriptive one-sided p=.115); the strongest vmPFC voxel had whole-brain adjusted p=.782.

Across the brain, 24 voxels survived correction, with the peak in posterior cerebellum (t=6.62, FWE p=.0007). This is not evidence for the vmPFC hypothesis. The interval allows small positive or negative effects, so lack of corrected support does not establish a zero effect.

The figures are [vmPFC effects](/Users/johnlee/code/CON/stamped-in-practice-workers/codex/narps-r1-12/results/group-analysis/vmPFC_effects.png) and [whole-brain corrected effects](/Users/johnlee/code/CON/stamped-in-practice-workers/codex/narps-r1-12/results/group-analysis/whole_brain_fwe.png).

## Checks and limitations

The analysis used 9,999 one-sided participant sign flips with whole-brain max-t correction. No participants were excluded. The common-support mask tested 198,029 voxels; it omitted 53,034 partially supported voxels, with the caveat that some exact-zero edge estimates could be genuine. The supplied cope maps are mean-centered, variance-normalized gain-modulator estimates, **not BOLD change per dollar**; the result is conditional on the fixed upstream model.
