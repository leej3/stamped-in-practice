Hypothesis conclusion: “The supplied group maps do not support a positive gain effect in vmPFC.”

The conclusion is defensible for the supplied participant-level gain-effect maps. The script includes all 54 equal-indifference participants, verifies finite maps on a common MNI152NLin2009cAsym grid, and uses a nonzero-coverage mask retaining voxels represented in at least 90% of maps. The equal-weight one-sample test is appropriate to independent participant effect estimates when participant variance maps are absent. A one-sided participant sign-flip max-T procedure controls whole-mask family-wise error, and the report distinguishes the corrected whole-brain result from its descriptive sphere estimate. The reported 24 corrected voxels form small posterior clusters (peak t(53)=6.62, max-T adjusted p=.001); the 10-mm sphere centered at [0, 44, −10] mm has mean −0.013 map units (95% t interval −0.044 to 0.018), and no corrected voxel overlaps it. These values and decisions are inspectable in `content/workspace/report.md`, `content/workspace/code/group_analysis.py`, `content/workspace/results/summary.json`, `content/workspace/results/fwe_clusters.tsv`, and `content/workspace/results/participant_qc_and_roi.tsv` (canonical capture SHA-256 `44b45c2d1e80cd01a5c6725623e8bffd63b3732ef2c1f010cdceea08c89f6878`).

The conclusion should remain qualified. The 90% mask is inferred from nonzero map coverage rather than a supplied independent brain mask, and sign-flip inference depends on participant-level sign exchangeability under the null. The vmPFC sphere is a declared coordinate-based operational definition, not a supplied anatomical ROI; the report correctly uses it descriptively and bases its decision on whole-brain correction. The maps are normalized-modulator effect estimates, not currency-unit slopes, and are conditional on the fixed upstream model. The absence of corrected vmPFC evidence is not evidence of equivalence or proof that vmPFC does not encode gain. The scientific result is therefore supported within the stated analysis and inputs, with these inferential limits.
