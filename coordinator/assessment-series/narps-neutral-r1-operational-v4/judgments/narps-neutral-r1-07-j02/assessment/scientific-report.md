## Scientific assessment

**Scientific adequacy: supported.** The report's no-support conclusion for a positive vmPFC gain effect is supported by a defensible group analysis and an appropriately corrected whole-brain test. The reported hypothesis outcome is “We found no support for a positive gain effect in vmPFC in the equal-indifference group under whole-brain familywise correction.” This is a conclusion about evidence under the specified model, not evidence that the true vmPFC effect is exactly zero.

The worker analyzed all 54 equal-indifference participants as independent second-level units, joined by participant ID to one map per person. The input COPEs are participant contrast estimates, not t/z statistics or signal change per currency unit; the supplied upstream methods say the gain regressor was mean-centered and variance-normalized and that separate gain/loss models were used. The report correctly limits interpretation to that conditional, fixed upstream estimand. Evidence: input/study-task.md (SHA-256 e92bf2e0c0db4cc49688cce9f9dfff8a236fc4ec151c09961719f49f31310cc8), input/upstream-methods.md (508d832c8787ed8303c3de101c2c76f40a3aa5dec53e6993dc2a49c4b7bcf90b), and scientific-report.md (9573bd34e4db368eb719e949095019e17dcaf345ab6d8ce5d442bab0378384d0).

The primary model is a one-sample t test across participants with a one-sided positive whole-brain max-T sign-flip permutation correction (9,999 sign patterns, fixed seed, FWE α=.05). Its exchangeability/symmetry assumption is stated. The mask uses the matching asymmetric MNI152NLin2009c brain mask resampled to the participant grid, intersected with finite nonzero support in every map; the report quantifies retained coverage (186,192/196,466 template-mask voxels, 94.8%) and the consequence that genuine zero estimates could be excluded. It checks map IDs, geometry, checksums and nonfinite values. The broad, explicit vmPFC midline box is used descriptively while the confirmatory decision remains whole-brain corrected. The report distinguishes a posterior whole-brain finding from vmPFC and states that missing participant variance maps limit precision modeling. Evidence: research-protocol.md (fd82723ae27072a2dc5c3077756192457bcbc11e9b9a80eb378c33af0a9541c3), analysis/analyze.py (a881abeea818018c57b767504d18472867da80fac8b13dd8a93d34d2a457dde2), and results/primary_v2_template_mask/summary.json (81e50cce17229e0eed5a518b845355616a167eec507e5b8696a4474d8d093488). The reported vmPFC mean is −0.0026 COPE units (95% t interval −0.0219 to 0.0167; n=54), with descriptive one-sided uncorrected p=.606; its most positive voxel has whole-brain max-T FWE p=.7742. No vmPFC voxel survives the stated whole-brain correction. The report therefore supports “no support” without treating a null result as proof of no effect.
