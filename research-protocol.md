# Group analysis protocol

**Question.** In the NARPS equal-indifference group, is the mean participant gain-effect estimate positive at any location in the brain, consistent with greater response as potential gain increases?

**Population and unit.** All 54 equal-indifference participants listed in `input/participants.tsv`; participant is the independent unit. No exclusions are planned. The cohort table is joined to one `sub-*_hypo1_cope.nii.gz` map by exact participant ID.

**Estimand.** Voxelwise group mean of AO86's supplied participant gain-modulator contrast parameter estimates (COPEs), in their native contrast units. They are not standardized statistics, and are not interpretable as signal change per currency unit: the upstream gain regressor was mean-centered and variance-normalized before HRF convolution. Gain effects are conditional on the separate gain/loss modeling choices in `input/upstream-methods.md`.

**Primary model and test.** At each voxel, a one-sample random-effects t model of the 54 participant COPEs against zero. Test only the positive direction (greater response) using sign-flip permutation of whole participant maps and a maximum t statistic across the complete analysis mask, controlling family-wise error at .05 across the brain. Use 9,999 random sign patterns and a fixed seed. The final analysis mask is the MNI152NLin2009cAsym res-02 template brain mask, nearest-neighbor resampled to the supplied image grid and intersected with finite, nonzero support in every map. This tests a population mean under exchangeability/symmetry of participant effects under the null. It is not a variance-weighted mixed-effects model because participant variance maps are unavailable.

**vmPFC localization.** The primary decision is whole-brain corrected, not an ROI-only test. For a transparent descriptive summary, operationalize medial vmPFC as the midline MNI box x = -10 to +10, y = 20 to 60, z = -20 to +20 mm, intersected with the analysis mask. Report its average participant effect and uncorrected one-sample interval/test as descriptive only; it does not supply confirmatory evidence. Plot unthresholded t values with the corrected result identified separately.

**Checks and uncertainty.** Verify IDs, file checksums, image shape/affine, finite support and map distributions; retain exclusions (if any) and missingness. Report the group mean, standard error, interval and one-sided whole-brain corrected p-values. As an independent numerical check, recompute a representative voxel's t and raw p with SciPy and compare to the image-derived result. Failure to reject does not establish no effect; with n=54 and absent first-level variances it may reflect limited precision or upstream model limitations.

**Method revision after pilot inspection.** Initial pilot `results/pilot_shared_support/` and `figures/pilot_shared_support/` used only the intersection of map support. Visual inspection showed that some shared support was outside the anatomical brain. Before accepting the primary result, revised the mask to the matching asymmetric MNI2009c brain mask and regenerated a separately named analysis derivative. The mask and T1 reference are the TemplateFlow MNI152NLin2009cAsym res-02 files, checksummed in the result summary; the mask is available from <https://templateflow.s3.amazonaws.com/tpl-MNI152NLin2009cAsym/tpl-MNI152NLin2009cAsym_res-02_desc-brain_mask.nii.gz>.

**Inputs and environment.** Fixed input bytes and their SHA256 identities are in `input/sources.json`. Do not rerun preprocessing or participant models or fetch time series. Use the supplied Python 3.13 environment with NumPy, SciPy, pandas, NiBabel and Matplotlib. Entry point: `python analysis/analyze.py` (or the prepared interpreter path in the README).
