## Scientific adequacy: qualified

The narrow conclusion that these inputs and this group model provide no whole-brain-corrected support for a positive vmPFC response is defensible. All 54 participants were analyzed with no exclusions. The script checked participant/map matching, common image geometry, finite values and input checksums; it used a 90%-coverage search mask (210,968 voxels) and one-sided participant sign-flip max-T correction across that whole mask (9,999 permutations, α=.05). It found 24 corrected voxels in three small posterior occipital components and none in the independently specified 10-mm vmPFC sphere centered at MNI [0, 42, -8]. The descriptive sphere mean was -0.0095 map units (95% t interval [-0.0377, 0.0187]). The relevant results are in [summary.json](/Users/johnlee/.local/state/stamped-in-practice/narps-v1/narps-r1-03/targets/judgment-01/results/summary.json), [report.md](/Users/johnlee/.local/state/stamped-in-practice/narps-v1/narps-r1-03/targets/judgment-01/results/report.md), and the replay figures below. This follows the supplied requirement for whole-brain correction in the [NARPS Hypothesis 1 instructions](https://www.narps.info/files/narps_instructions_for_analysis_teams.pdf).

The result is a lack of evidence for the predicted response under the supplied estimates and model, not evidence that the true vmPFC effect is zero or that vmPFC does not represent gain. The sign-flip inference assumes a symmetric participant-level null. Participant-level variance maps were not supplied, so first-level uncertainty could not enter a precision-weighted or hierarchical group model. The coverage mask infers coverage from nonzero values. Upstream gain regressors were variance-normalized and gain and loss were modeled separately; the cope is not a per-currency-unit effect and does not isolate gain jointly from loss. The report states these limits. Figure 1 localizes the corrected posterior findings and the predefined sphere; Figure 2 shows all participant-level sphere estimates.
